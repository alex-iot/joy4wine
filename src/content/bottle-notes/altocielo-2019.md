I bought my first bottle of Altocielo on a local recommendation, and it didn’t go to plan. The cork was faulty. The wine was still drinkable, but the fruit was muted and most of the fun had gone out of it. Still, there was enough inside to make me curious, especially the structure and full body, which gave the wine a solid backbone. So I bought a second bottle to give it a fair chance. I’m glad I did: this time the full flavours unfolded to complement that backbone.

Altocielo is the flagship of Bodegas Lunares, a small family bodega just outside Ronda. The Morales family works four hectares there, and the wine takes its name from the vineyards: high sky, at around 900 metres, where hot days give way to cold nights.

It’s a big wine, and it doesn’t pretend otherwise. The label says 15%, and while the alcohol is well enough integrated, it sits on the warm side. There’s enough acidity to keep the whole thing lively. In the glass it’s deep purple, with an orange edge at the rim that shows its age.

The nose has a lot going on. Wild flowers first, then clove, black pepper and charred wood, over very ripe fruit: strawberry and plum, black plum and blackcurrant. A menthol, almost balsamic thread runs through it, and dark chocolate comes up as it opens.

On the palate it’s full and pronounced. The tannins are still high and grippy, but time has taken some of the edge off them, and they feel tamer than they would have a few years ago. The finish is medium-plus, the menthol and cocoa staying on a while after.

Seven years on, I was surprised by how well it has evolved. It’s in a good place now, but I wouldn’t hold on to it much longer. At the same time, I’d have liked to try it a bit younger, to see where it started from. Give it some air and pour it with something that can stand up to it. Rabo de toro would do nicely with it.
